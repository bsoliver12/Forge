Why the week is laid out this way:
  • Every muscle gets trained twice a week. Upper and lower each have an A and a B session three days apart (Mon→Thu, Tue→Fri). Four days at twice-a-week frequency keeps weekly sets per muscle close to the old five-day split (a little lower in Phase 1, back up in Phase 2) with one less session to recover from.
  • Still two gym trips. Tuesday (leg press and hack squat) and Thursday (cables and machines) are the two sessions that actually need the gym. Monday, Wednesday and Friday run at home.
  • Hard intervals get their own day. Wednesday sits between Tuesday's legs and Thursday's upper session, and two days ahead of Friday's hinge work, so the hard cardio never lands the day before a leg day.
  • Saturday stays open for meets. Running a meet means hours on your feet. That counts as activity, so nothing gets added on top of it.
⚑ Season rules — how to keep the week intact:
  • Meet on a weekday? Move that day's session to Saturday. The exception is a Tuesday meet: do Lower A on Wednesday and move the intervals to Saturday. The rule underneath both: never two lower-body days back to back.
  • Running with the team? Easy team runs count as Zone 2. If you run a workout with them (repeats, tempo), it replaces Wednesday's intervals that week. Don't do both.
  • Rough week (bad sleep, travel, long meet days): cut every exercise to 2 sets and keep the loads. One lighter week costs almost nothing. Grinding through it can cost you the next two.
  • Missed a session: skip it and pick up with the next one in order. Don't double up.
Right-arm rule for this block (the tendon is better but still flares): Both arms get direct biceps work again, neutral grip only (DB hammer curl on Monday, single-arm cable hammer curl on Thursday). The right arm starts at about half of the left arm's load and moves up one step only after two sessions in a row that pass the pain check. Pain check: no more than 3/10 during the set, and back to your normal baseline by the next morning. A flare is next-morning pain up 2+ points, or pain with everyday tasks. When that happens, drop right-side curls for the rest of the week and restart one step lower. Clicking, catching or sudden weakness means stop direct arm work and get it examined. Everything excluded before stays excluded: barbell bench, barbell or palms-up curls, chin-ups, dips, behind-the-neck work, and mixed grip on any pull. The full steps are in the Shoulder Rehab tab.
How loads go up — double progression: Every exercise has a rep range. Stay at a load until every set hits the top of the range at the target RPE. Then make the smallest jump available the next session (DB +5 lb, machine +1 plate, barbell +5 lb upper / +10 lb lower) and work back up from the bottom of the range. The one exception is the DB flat press, where your heaviest dumbbells cap the load. Progress that one with the techniques in Monday's box.

NAD⁺
25 mg — Subcutaneous Injection, 3×/week
⚑ Pharmacology correction: the common claim that subQ NAD⁺ delivers intact NAD⁺ "for direct cellular uptake" is not well-supported. NAD⁺ is a large, charged molecule that mostly does not cross plasma membranes intact — circulating NAD⁺ is largely broken down extracellularly to precursors (NMN → NR → nicotinamide) that are then taken up and re-synthesized inside cells. So subQ NAD⁺ likely works through those precursors, not by direct repletion, and the flushing is a real (dose-limiting) effect. It's not useless, but it's the least evidence-supported NAD⁺ route in your stack. If the goal is raising NAD⁺, oral nicotinamide riboside (NR) has the strongest human data (dose-dependent blood-NAD⁺ rises of ~22/51/142% at 100/300/1000 mg over two weeks, plus evidence of raising brain NAD⁺) — a more defensible spend than injectable NAD⁺.
